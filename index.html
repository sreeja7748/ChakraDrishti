What the numbers actually show

Resonance, not intelligence

Round-robin's 33.5% looks decent, but it's a coincidence: its 8-tick sweep period happens to line up with a periodic emitter's own 8-tick cycle. Change that emitter's timing slightly and the advantage disappears.

Bandits learn averages, not timing

Multi-armed bandits (Epsilon-Greedy, UCB) track each band's average hit rate but have no notion of when — so they can't exploit a band that's only hot for 2 out of every 8 ticks. That's a real, structural blind spot, not a tuning issue.

Trained scheduler: 60.7%

A Q-learning agent conditioned on time-in-cycle discovered the emitter's exact schedule from hits and misses alone — catching both ticks of its active window instead of one, nearly 2x round-robin.

No free lunch

That 60.7% took 500 practice episodes offline. A period-discovery scheduler that learns live, in a single encounter with zero prior training, still reaches 41.4% — a real tradeoff between training cost and one-shot adaptability.
